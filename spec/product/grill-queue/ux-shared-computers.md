# Shared front-desk computers

## Decide

- Whether the screen locks or hides patient details after a period without use, and how long.
- Whether staff can switch user quickly on a shared computer, and what the next person sees.
- What happens to the previous person's open drafts and screens when the session locks or
  someone else signs in.
- Whether anything differs for screens in public view, such as reception, from those in
  consulting rooms.

## Why we're asking

Reception computers are shared and often visible to waiting patients. If a receptionist steps
away mid-booking, the screen may show a patient's details to the room, and the next staff
member may act under the wrong name, which the [audit requirements](../../cross-cutting/audit/)
depend on being right. Locking too aggressively loses work and slows the desk. This touches
[authentication](../../cross-cutting/authentication/) and
[privacy](../../cross-cutting/privacy/) as well as screen design.

## Examples

- A receptionist steps away for two minutes with a patient's contact details on screen. What
  does the waiting room see?
- A second receptionist sits down at the same computer and needs to book a patient now. Do they
  sign in over the first person's session?
- A nurse's session locks while they are halfway through recording observations. When they
  come back, is the draft there?
