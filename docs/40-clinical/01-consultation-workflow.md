# The Consultation

**Status:** `modelled`

## Purpose

This is the workflow the entire product exists to serve. It is also the one most software gets
wrong, by treating the consultation as a form to fill in rather than a conversation to support.

The design principle here: **the software follows the consultation the GP was trained to conduct.
It never leads it.**

## Who does it

GP, GP Registrar, Nurse Practitioner. Practice Nurse for nurse-led encounters.

## What GPs are taught

Two frameworks, both taught in Australian GP training, both reflected in the design.

### The phases of the consultation

1. **Initiating** — establish rapport, identify the reason(s) for attending
2. **Gathering information** — history, exploring the patient's ideas, concerns and expectations
3. **Physical examination**
4. **Explanation and planning** — shared decision-making
5. **Closing** — summarise, agree the plan, and **safety-net**

### Murtagh's safe diagnostic strategy

For each presenting problem, five questions:

1. What is the **probability diagnosis**?
2. What **serious disorders must not be missed**?
3. What conditions are **often missed** (the pitfalls)?
4. Could this be one of the **masquerades**? — depression, diabetes, drugs, anaemia, thyroid
   disease, spinal dysfunction, urinary tract infection
5. **Is the patient trying to tell me something else?**

This structure is examined in RACGP Fellowship assessment. Building it into the note as *optional
scaffolding* is what makes this software useful to a registrar and unobtrusive to a GP of 20
years.

## The workflow

### Before the patient sits down

Opening the encounter presents a **pre-consultation summary** the GP can absorb in five seconds:

- Reason for today's visit (from booking) and any triage notes
- Alerts: allergies and adverse reactions first, then clinical alerts
- Active problems
- Current medicines, with anything due for review flagged
- Last visit: when, who, what for
- **Outstanding items**: unactioned results, open recalls, referrals without a reply, care plan
  review due, screening due, immunisations due
- MyMedicare and entitlement status (because it changes what can be offered)

### During

The note is a single scrolling document, not a wizard, with these regions:

| Region | Content |
|---|---|
| **Reason for encounter** | What the patient came for, in their words where possible |
| **History** | Free text, with optional structured prompts |
| **Examination** | Free text plus structured observations (BP, HR, temp, weight, height, BMI, SpO₂, BGL, peak flow) |
| **Assessment** | Free text plus **coded conditions** added to the problem list |
| **Plan** | Free text plus the actions taken (scripts, requests, referrals, certificates, recalls) |
| **Safety-netting** | What to watch for, what to do, when to come back — a distinct field |

**Murtagh scaffolding** is a collapsible panel per problem: probability diagnosis, must-not-miss,
often-missed, masquerades considered, patient's own agenda. Off by default for fellowed GPs; on by
default for registrars; configurable per practitioner.

**Actions taken inside the note** — each one opens inline and returns to the note:

- Prescribe → [03-prescribing.md](03-prescribing.md)
- Request pathology or imaging → [04-investigations.md](04-investigations.md)
- Refer → [06-referrals.md](06-referrals.md)
- Start or review a care plan → [07-chronic-condition-management.md](07-chronic-condition-management.md)
- Record an immunisation → [09-immunisation.md](09-immunisation.md)
- Issue a certificate or letter → [12-certificates-and-reports.md](12-certificates-and-reports.md)
- Create a recall → [05-results-and-recalls.md](05-results-and-recalls.md)
- Perform a procedure → recorded with consent, site, technique, consumables, and outcome

### Closing

1. **Safety-netting is prompted** if the field is empty and the encounter involved a new problem
   or a diagnostic uncertainty. Not blocked — prompted. Blocking a GP from finishing a note is
   how you get "asdf" typed into fields.
2. **Follow-up**: book now, or create a recall/reminder.
3. **Billing**: the item(s) are proposed from the encounter — duration for the time tier, plus any
   procedural or care-planning items generated by what happened. The GP confirms.
4. **Sign the note.** Signing makes it immutable; later changes are amendments.

### Handover and continuity (C5.3, GP2.1)

If the patient's usual GP is someone else, the note is flagged for their awareness where the
encounter involved: a new significant diagnosis, a medication change, a referral, or an abnormal
result. This is a lightweight continuity mechanism, not a task queue — it appears in the usual
GP's "patients seen by others" list.

## Rules and constraints

1. A signed note is immutable. Corrections are `note_amendment` records with author, time and
   reason; the original stays readable.
2. A note cannot be signed without: reason for encounter, and either an assessment or a documented
   reason there isn't one.
3. Every prescription, request, referral and certificate is bound to an encounter — no orphan
   clinical actions.
4. The encounter duration is recorded automatically from `in_consultation` to `completed`, and
   used to *suggest* the MBS time tier. It never bills automatically.
5. Allergy and adverse reaction data must be current or explicitly marked "not known" — the
   consultation header shows which.
6. Registrar notes flagged `supervisor_review_requested` appear in the supervisor's list and
   record when they were reviewed and by whom.

## Data touched

`encounters`, `clinical_notes`, `note_amendments`, `conditions`, `observations`,
`prescriptions`, `investigation_requests`, `referrals`, `care_plans`, `immunisations`,
`recalls`, `documents`, `invoices`, `audit_log_entries`.

## Offline behaviour

**Fully supported for note-taking.** Drafting and signing work offline. Actions that need an
external exchange — issuing an eScript, checking RTPM, sending a referral by secure messaging —
are disabled offline with a plain explanation, and the intended action can be queued as a task so
it isn't forgotten on return.
